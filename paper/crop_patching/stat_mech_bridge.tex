\textbf{From the model to interventions.} The model separates two experimental handles: changing an agent's private evidence and changing the social evidence it receives. We examine both in Sec.~\ref{sec:causal-interventions}, using a memory-conflict probe and a private-crop patching experiment. Improving a crop can strengthen support for the truth, but does not literally clamp the agent into the model's zealot state: its subsequent responses remain free to change. The model also motivates distinct tests of removing a unique evidence source, introducing a persistent rival, changing the prevalence of diagnostic crops, or changing the communication rule. These predictions are separate from the crop-patching results reported below.
